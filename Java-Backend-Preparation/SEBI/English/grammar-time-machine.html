What is a "tense"?

Tense just means WHEN something happens. Did it happen already? Is it happening right now? Or will it happen later? English has words that change a little bit depending on the answer — and that's all a tense really is!

Click any card below to jump straight into that tense. Each one has a picture, easy examples, and a fun game to practice!

🌞

Present Simple

Things that happen again and again and again!

😊 What is it?

We use the Present Simple for things that happen all the time — habits, routines, and facts that are always true. Think of the sun: it rises every single day. That's a job for Present Simple!

🤔 When do we use it?

  • Habits & routines: "I brush my teeth every morning."
  • Facts that are always true: "Water boils at 100°C."
  • Things we do regularly: "She plays the piano on Fridays."
  • Schedules and timetables: "The train leaves at 6 PM."

🧩 How do we build it?

I / You / We / They + verb (base form)
He / She / It + verb + s / es

👉 The little trick to remember: he, she, it — add an -s! "He plays", "She runs", "It barks".

Negative: I don't play. He doesn't play.   Question: Do you play? Does he play?

⏳ Where does it live on the timeline?

🔑 Signal words (clue words!)

alwaysusuallyoften sometimesneverevery day every weekon Mondays

📚 Examples

I walk to school every day.
The sun rises in the east.
My dog barks at the postman.
We eat dinner at 7 o'clock.
She doesn't like spicy food.
Cats love milk.

Present Continuous

Something is happening RIGHT NOW, this very second!

😊 What is it?

Imagine taking a photo of yourself right now. What are you doing in that photo? Reading? Sitting? That's Present Continuous — it snaps a picture of an action happening at this exact moment, or something temporary happening around now (even if you're not doing it this very second).

🤔 When do we use it?

  • Happening right now: "I am reading this sentence."
  • Temporary situations (true these days, not forever): "She is staying with her aunt this month."
  • Changing or developing situations: "The weather is getting colder."
  • Future plans already arranged: "We are meeting Grandma tomorrow."

🧩 How do we build it?

Subject + am / is / are + verb + ing

👉 I am playing. He/She/It is playing. We/You/They are playing.

Negative: I am not playing.   Question: Are you playing?

⏳ Where does it live on the timeline?

🔑 Signal words (clue words!)

nowright nowat the moment Look!Listen!these days currently

📚 Examples

Look! It is raining.
I am doing my homework right now.
They are playing in the garden.
She is writing a letter at the moment.
We are not watching TV right now.
The baby is sleeping.

Present Perfect

It already happened — and it still matters right now!

😊 What is it?

Imagine you finish your homework. It happened in the past, but the important thing is right now: you're free to play! Present Perfect connects something that already happened to how things are now. We don't say exactly when it happened — just that it did, and it matters today.

🤔 When do we use it?

  • A finished action with a result now: "I have lost my keys." (so now I can't get in!)
  • Life experience (ever, at some point in life): "She has visited Japan."
  • Something that started in the past and continues now: "They have lived here since 2015."
  • Recently completed actions: "We have just finished lunch."

🧩 How do we build it?

Subject + have / has + past participle

👉 Past participle is the "3rd form" of the verb — like gone, seen, eaten, played, finished. I have eaten. He has eaten.

Negative: I haven't eaten.   Question: Have you eaten? Has she eaten?

⏳ Where does it live on the timeline?

🔑 Signal words (clue words!)

alreadyyetjust everneversince 2015 for 3 yearsso far

📚 Examples

I have finished my homework.
She has never been to Japan.
Have you ever eaten sushi?
He has lost his keys again!
We have lived here since 2015.
They have just arrived.

Past Simple

A story that already happened — and it's completely finished!

😊 What is it?

Past Simple is for telling a finished story — like something you read in a book that already ended. "The dragon flew away." It happened, it's over, done and dusted!

🤔 When do we use it?

  • A finished action at a known past time: "I visited my grandma yesterday."
  • A series of finished actions: "She woke up, brushed her teeth, and left."
  • Something that used to happen but doesn't anymore: "He played football when he was young."

🧩 How do we build it?

Subject + verb + ed (or irregular form)

👉 Regular verbs add -ed: play → played. But lots of verbs are cheeky and change completely: go → went, eat → ate, see → saw. These are called irregular verbs — you just have to remember them!

Negative: I didn't play (verb goes back to base form!).   Question: Did you play?

⏳ Where does it live on the timeline?

🔑 Signal words (clue words!)

yesterdaylast weeklast night 2 days agoin 2010when I was young

📚 Examples

I went to the park yesterday.
She made a cake last night.
They didn't watch the movie.
Did you see that bird?
He broke his leg.
We lived in Mumbai two years ago.

Past Continuous

Like a movie playing in the past — and then, something happened!

😊 What is it?

Imagine a movie playing... and suddenly the film gets interrupted! Past Continuous shows an action in progress at a moment in the past — often right when something else happened and cut in.

🤔 When do we use it?

  • An action interrupted by another action: "I was watching TV when the phone rang."
  • Two things happening at the same time in the past: "She was cooking while he was cleaning."
  • Setting the scene of a story: "It was raining and the wind was blowing."

🧩 How do we build it?

Subject + was / were + verb + ing

👉 I/He/She/It was playing. We/You/They were playing.

Negative: I wasn't playing.   Question: Were you playing?

⏳ Where does it live on the timeline?

🔑 Signal words (clue words!)

whilewhenat that moment at 8 o'clock last nightall day yesterday

📚 Examples

I was watching TV when the phone rang.
While she was cooking, the doorbell rang.
They were playing football at 5 PM.
It was raining when we left the house.
What were you doing at 8 last night?
The kids weren't sleeping when I checked.

Past Perfect

The "past before the past" — it happened even earlier!

😊 What is it?

Imagine two things happened in the past — but one happened before the other. Past Perfect is like a time machine that goes back to show which one came first. "When I arrived, the movie had already started." (The movie started FIRST, then I arrived.)

🤔 When do we use it?

  • The earlier of two past actions: "She had left before I called."
  • Explaining why something happened: "He was tired because he had studied all night."
  • Life experiences, before a certain past point: "I had never seen snow before that trip."

🧩 How do we build it?

Subject + had + past participle

👉 Had is the magic word — it's the same for everyone! I had gone. She had gone. They had gone.

Negative: I hadn't gone.   Question: Had you gone?

⏳ Where does it live on the timeline?

🔑 Signal words (clue words!)

beforeafteralready by the timenever...before

📚 Examples

By the time we arrived, the movie had started.
She had never seen snow before that trip.
The train had already left.
He was tired because he had studied all night.
After they had finished lunch, they went for a walk.
The guests had left before I got home.

Future Forms

Things that haven't happened yet — but will!

😊 What is it?

The future hasn't happened yet, so English gives us three different tools to talk about it, depending on how sure or planned it is. Let's meet all three!

1️⃣ WILL — for promises, quick decisions, and predictions (just a guess)

Subject + will + verb (base form)
  • Promise: "I will help you tomorrow."
  • Deciding right this second: "The phone is ringing — I will get it!"
  • A guess about the future: "I think it will rain later."

2️⃣ GOING TO — for plans already decided, or things you can see coming

Subject + am / is / are going to + verb
  • A plan you've already made: "She is going to study medicine."
  • Evidence right in front of you: "Look at those clouds — it is going to rain!"

3️⃣ Present Continuous — for fixed arrangements (tickets booked, plans set!)

Subject + am / is / are + verb + ing
  • Already arranged, like a ticket booked: "We are going to Goa next month."

⏳ Where does it live on the timeline?

🔑 Signal words (clue words!)

tomorrownext weeksoon I promiseI thinkLook!

📚 Examples

Look at those clouds! It is going to rain.
I will help you carry that bag.
We are going to Goa next month.
She is going to study medicine.
I promise I will call you tomorrow.
Careful, you are going to fall!
