01

Noun

the naming word — person, place, thing, or idea
Definition: A noun names a person, place, thing, animal, or idea. If you can put "the" or "a" in front of it, or make it plural, it's almost certainly a noun.

Field marks — types of nouns

Common
A general, non-specific name.
city, dog, teacher
Proper
A specific name, always capitalized.
Chennai, Priya, Monday
Abstract
Something you can't touch — an idea or quality.
honesty, freedom, joy
Concrete
Something you can perceive with your senses.
table, rain, music
Collective
Names a group as a single unit.
team, flock, family
Countable / Uncountable
Can it be counted (books) or not (water)?
apples vs. rice

In the wild

  • The teacher praised the student's effort.
  • Freedom is something every nation values.
  • The committee announced its decision in Chennai.

Practice — spot the noun

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Underline all nouns: "The children played happily in the garden near the old temple."

Answer: children, garden, temple

2. Which word is an abstract noun? chair  |  courage  |  river  |  mango

Answer: courage — it names a quality you can't touch or see.

3. Fill the blank with a collective noun: A of wolves hunted at night.

Answer: pack (a "pack of wolves")

4. Identify the proper noun: "My friend Aisha visited Paris last summer."

Answer: Aisha, Paris — both are specific names, so both are capitalized.

5. Is "water" countable or uncountable in "I drank two glasses of water"?

Answer: Uncountable — you count the "glasses," not the "waters." Water itself has no plural.
02

Pronoun

a stand-in for a noun, so we don't repeat ourselves
Definition: A pronoun replaces a noun already mentioned (its "antecedent") so sentences don't sound repetitive. Instead of "Ravi took Ravi's bag," we say "Ravi took his bag."

Field marks — types of pronouns

Personal
Stand in for specific people or things.
I, you, he, she, it, we, they
Possessive
Show ownership.
mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs
Reflexive
Action reflects back on the subject.
myself, yourself, themselves
Demonstrative
Point to something specific.
this, that, these, those
Interrogative
Used to ask questions.
who, whom, whose, which, what
Relative
Introduce a clause describing a noun.
who, which, that, whose
Indefinite
Refer to non-specific people/things.
someone, anything, everybody, few

In the wild

  • She gave herself credit for this.
  • Someone left their umbrella, and it isn't mine.
  • The man who called earlier is waiting for you.

Practice — spot the pronoun

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Replace the repeated noun with a pronoun: "Meera lost Meera's phone, so Meera borrowed a phone from a friend."

Answer: "Meera lost her phone, so she borrowed one from a friend."

2. Which type is "themselves"? possessive | reflexive | relative

Answer: reflexive — the action bounces back onto the subject.

3. Choose correctly: "Is this book (your/yours)?"

Answer: yours — a possessive pronoun standing alone (not before a noun).

4. Find the relative pronoun: "The recipe that my grandmother wrote is still in the drawer."

Answer: that — it introduces a clause describing "the recipe."

5. "Everybody enjoyed the show" — what type of pronoun is "everybody"?

Answer: indefinite pronoun — it refers to people without naming anyone specific.
03

Verb

the action or state — every sentence needs one
Definition: A verb shows an action (run, think, build) or a state of being (is, seem, become). Every complete sentence needs at least one verb.

Field marks — types of verbs

Action
Something physical or mental happens.
run, write, imagine
Linking
Connects subject to a description; no action.
is, seem, become, feel
Transitive
Takes a direct object — action passes to something.
She reads a book.
Intransitive
No direct object needed.
The baby slept.
Auxiliary (helping)
Supports the main verb — tense, mood, voice.
is, has, will, can, must
Modal
Expresses ability, permission, possibility.
can, could, may, should

In the wild

  • She writes a letter every week.
  • He seemed tired after the journey.
  • They have been waiting since morning.

Practice — spot the verb

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Identify the verb: "The chef prepared a delicious meal."

Answer: prepared — an action verb.

2. Is "is" here linking or action? "The soup is delicious."

Answer: Linking — it connects "soup" to the description "delicious," with no action happening.

3. Transitive or intransitive: "The children laughed."

Answer: Intransitive — "laughed" doesn't act on any object.

4. Pick the correct modal: "You (must/mustn't) wear a helmet — it's the law."

Answer: must — expresses an obligation.

5. Find the auxiliary verb: "She has finished her homework."

Answer: has — it helps form the present perfect tense with "finished."
04

Adjective

describes or qualifies a noun or pronoun
Definition: An adjective adds detail to a noun or pronoun — size, color, quantity, quality, or which one. Ask "what kind?", "how many?", or "which one?" — the answer is usually an adjective.

Field marks — types of adjectives

Descriptive
Tells what kind or quality.
tall, blue, ancient
Quantitative
Tells how much or how many.
some, few, three, all
Demonstrative
Points to a specific noun.
this, that, these, those
Possessive
Shows ownership before a noun.
my, your, their
Comparative / Superlative
Compares two, or three or more things.
taller / tallest
Proper
Formed from a proper noun.
Indian, Victorian

In the wild

  • The ancient, weathered temple stood on a quiet hill.
  • She is taller than her older brother.
  • Three students finished the difficult exam early.

Practice — spot the adjective

click "reveal" after you answer

1. List the adjectives: "The small, curious kitten chased a red ball."

Answer: small, curious, red

2. Give the comparative form of "good."

Answer: better (irregular comparative; superlative is "best").

3. Correct the error: "This is the (most best / best) film I've seen."

Answer: best — never combine "most" with an already-superlative form.

4. Which word is a possessive adjective? "I found their keys near your car."

Answer: their, your — both modify a noun to show ownership.

5. "Few people knew the answer" — what kind of adjective is "few"?

Answer: Quantitative — it tells how many people.
05

Adverb

modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb
Definition: An adverb adds detail to a verb, adjective, or another adverb — usually answering how, when, where, how often, or to what degree. Many (not all) end in -ly.

Field marks — types of adverbs

Manner
How something happens.
quickly, carefully, well
Place
Where something happens.
here, outside, nearby
Time
When something happens.
yesterday, soon, now
Frequency
How often something happens.
always, often, rarely
Degree
How much or to what extent.
very, quite, almost

In the wild

  • She sang beautifully and confidently.
  • He always arrives early.
  • The tea was extremely hot.

Practice — spot the adverb

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Identify the adverb: "He solved the puzzle easily."

Answer: easily — describes how he solved it (manner).

2. What does "extremely" modify in "The test was extremely hard"?

Answer: the adjective "hard" — "extremely" is a degree adverb.

3. Choose the frequency adverb: quickly | rarely | outside | yesterday

Answer: rarely

4. Fix the word order: "She (well plays / plays well) the piano."

Answer: plays well — manner adverbs usually follow the verb (or its object).

5. Is "fast" an adjective or adverb in "He is a fast runner" vs. "He runs fast"?

Answer: In the first, "fast" describes the noun "runner" (adjective). In the second, it describes the verb "runs" (adverb) — same word, different job.
06

Preposition

shows the relationship between a noun and something else
Definition: A preposition sits before a noun or pronoun to show its relationship to another word — often location, time, or direction. It's always followed by an "object" (a noun/pronoun), never a verb.

Field marks — types of prepositions

Time
Places an event in time.
at, on, in, since, during
Place
Shows physical position.
in, on, at, under, between
Direction
Shows movement toward something.
to, into, toward, across
Agent / Instrument
Shows who or what did something.
by, with

In the wild

  • The keys are on the table, under a notebook.
  • We arrived at the station before sunrise.
  • She walked into the room and sat beside him.

Practice — spot the preposition

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Fill in the correct time preposition: "The meeting is (in/on/at) 5 o'clock."

Answer: at — use "at" for exact clock times ("in" for months/years, "on" for days/dates).

2. Identify all prepositions: "The cat jumped over the fence and hid under the porch."

Answer: over, under

3. Choose correctly: "She has lived here (since/for) 2015."

Answer: since — use "since" with a starting point in time, "for" with a duration (e.g., "for 9 years").

4. "The letter was written by my grandfather" — what kind of preposition is "by" here?

Answer: Agent — it shows who performed the action in a passive sentence.

5. Spot the error: "I am good in painting." What's the natural preposition?

Answer: "good at painting" — certain adjectives pair with fixed prepositions; these must be learned individually.
07

Conjunction

joins words, phrases, or clauses together
Definition: A conjunction connects words, phrases, or clauses. The seven coordinating conjunctions spell FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So.

Field marks — types of conjunctions

Coordinating
Joins equal elements (FANBOYS).
and, but, or, so
Subordinating
Joins a dependent clause to a main clause.
because, although, if, since
Correlative
Work in pairs.
either...or, neither...nor, both...and

In the wild

  • I wanted to go, but it was raining.
  • Although he was tired, he finished the race.
  • Either you apologize or you leave.

Practice — spot the conjunction

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Join the sentences with a suitable conjunction: "It was cold. We went for a swim."

Answer: "It was cold, but we went for a swim." (contrast → "but")

2. Name the FANBOYS conjunction that shows a reason: "She left early, for she had a train to catch."

Answer: for — here it means "because."

3. Complete the correlative pair: " Ravi his sister speaks French." (neither / nor)

Answer: Neither Ravi nor his sister speaks French.

4. Is "because" coordinating or subordinating?

Answer: Subordinating — it attaches a dependent (incomplete) clause to a main clause.

5. Choose the right word: "I was hungry, (so/yet) I made a sandwich."

Answer: so — it shows a result/consequence.
08

Interjection

a burst of feeling, standing outside the grammar of the sentence
Definition: An interjection expresses sudden emotion — surprise, joy, pain, hesitation. It doesn't connect grammatically to the rest of the sentence and is often followed by an exclamation mark or a comma.

Field marks — types of interjections

Strong emotion
Usually stands alone with "!"
Wow! Ouch! Hooray!
Mild emotion
Blended into the sentence with a comma.
Well, Hmm, Oh
Greeting / Parting
Social exchanges.
Hi! Bye! Cheers!

In the wild

  • Ouch! That really hurt.
  • Well, I suppose we should get going.
  • Wow, this view is breathtaking!

Practice — spot the interjection

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Pick the interjection: "Hurray! We won the match."

Answer: Hurray — expresses sudden joy, punctuated with "!"

2. Add a suitable interjection for pain to: ", I stubbed my toe!"

Answer: Ouch (or "Ow")

3. Is "Hmm, let me think about that" mild or strong emotion?

Answer: Mild — it's hesitation, gently set off with a comma rather than an exclamation mark.
09

Article

a, an, the — small words that frame a noun
Definition: Articles are a special class of adjective that signal whether a noun is specific (the) or non-specific (a / an). English has only three: a definite article and two forms of the indefinite article.

Field marks — types of articles

Definite — "the"
A specific, already-known noun.
the book on my desk
Indefinite — "a"
Any one, before a consonant sound.
a university, a dog
Indefinite — "an"
Any one, before a vowel sound.
an hour, an apple

In the wild

  • I saw a cat sitting on the wall near an old gate.
  • She is the best player on the team.
  • He wants to become an engineer some day.

Practice — a / an / the

click "reveal" after you answer

1. Fill in: "She is honest person." (a/an)

Answer: an — "honest" starts with a vowel sound (silent h), so it takes "an."

2. Fill in: "He works as European ambassador." (a/an)

Answer: a — "European" starts with a "y" consonant sound, despite the vowel letter, so it takes "a."

3. Fill in: " sun rises in east." (the / the)

Answer: The sun rises in the east — unique things and cardinal directions take "the."

4. Should you use an article here? " Mount Everest is the tallest mountain."

Answer: No article — most proper nouns (names of single mountains, people, countries) take no article.

5. Fill in: "I waited for hour before bus arrived." (an / the)

Answer: an hour ... the bus — "hour" starts with a silent h (vowel sound); "the bus" refers to a specific, known bus.
